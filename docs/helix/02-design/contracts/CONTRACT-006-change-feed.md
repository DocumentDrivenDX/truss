# Contract: Change feed

**Contract ID**: CONTRACT-006
**Type**: schema
**Version**: layout 0.2 (draft)
**Status**: draft
**Related**: ADR-002 D7, D15, CONTRACT-001 (`feed_consumer`), CONTRACT-002 (journal, consumer watermark), CONTRACT-003 (catalog revision)

## Purpose

Defines what any publisher of truss's changes to a downstream copy (a warehouse, a search index, another database) must preserve, so that the copy is complete, correctly ordered, repeatable after a failure, and honest about how current it is. It states content and ordering. It does not choose a transport: a custom reader of the journal and a database-native synchronization are both allowed, provided they preserve what is stated here.

## Scope and Boundaries

- In scope: the records a feed carries, ordering, completeness, replay, the delivery guarantee, consumer positions, freshness evidence, and how retention interacts with consumers.
- Out of scope: the transport, the downstream table design, and what a consumer does with a record.
- Owning project: truss.

## Normative Surface

**Selected storage distinction (2026-10-07):** CONTRACT-012's 0.6 review profile integrates all four feed fact/prerequisite stores and the [complete consumer home](complete-feed-consumer-v0.1.proposal.sql). The baseline feed_consumer row below remains journal-only. Complete manifest/seed/coverage boundaries cannot be projected to (xid,seq). Native registration/fencing/full prior-boundary CAS, seed activation/administrative receipt persistence, guard bodies, roles and full conversion remain separate required outputs.


**Records.** A feed carries these record kinds, each defined by tables of CONTRACT-001.

| Kind | Source | Content |
|------|--------|---------|
| `change` | a `journal` row | entity kind, id and type, `ver`, `op`, `prop_id`, old and new value, `rev`, `origin` (CONTRACT-002), and its `(xid, seq)` position |
| `revision` | a `schema_rev` row with its `schema_doc` rows | `rev`, `accepted_at`, `origin`, and each document's identity, revision token, UMF version, `content_sha256` and bytes |
| `source` | a `record_source` row | entity kind and id, `load_id`, `source` |
| `reservation` | a `key_tombstone` row | entity kind, type, key number and key text, the entity that held it, and its `ver` |

A delete is a `change` with `op` `delete` that carries the record as it was, so a consumer needs no other record to remove it.

**Order.** `change` records are delivered in `(xid, seq)` order and only below the safe watermark of CONTRACT-002 (`xid < pg_snapshot_xmin(pg_current_snapshot())`), so no change that commits later can have a position before one already delivered. A `revision` record `N` is delivered before the first `change` whose `rev` is `N` or later. `source` and `reservation` records are delivered with, or after, the `change` of the transaction that wrote them.

**Completeness.** A forward scan from a fixed acknowledged boundary enumerates each eligible committed journal row once in position order, unless retention has removed it by explicit consumer consent (below). No rolled-back change is emitted. Transport retries and restarts may redeliver records: the delivery guarantee is at-least-once, with exactly-once downstream effects requiring durable idempotent application and acknowledgment. A publisher MUST NOT claim end-to-end exactly-once effects solely from ordered scan uniqueness.

**Authorization and scope.** A complete database feed requires explicitly authorized access to every required change, revision, source and reservation in its declared scope. Ordinary row-level-security visibility is not proof of complete eligibility: a query can silently omit forbidden rows. A publisher must qualify its scope against independently enumerated required events under authorized administrative test observation; it must not promote a role-filtered journal query to a complete database feed. Administrative observation in tests is evidence, not permission for production payload disclosure.

CONTRACT-005 governs historical payload access under current grants and retained ownership. If access to a required event is revoked or its ownership cannot be established, consumption stops before that event; a consumer cannot advance its applied checkpoint over it or report a complete replica. Publisher internals must not reveal hidden event contents or cross-scope existence through ordinary consumer diagnostics. Record a permitted scope-level unavailable/authorization status without leaking the hidden record. Grant restoration permits authorized resumption from the last applied boundary while retention still protects the outstanding event.

A filtered feed requires a separately versioned projection contract defining membership, catalog disclosure, grant-change behavior, removal/reseed semantics and its own checkpoint domain. Reusing the global position and dropping invisible records is insufficient: a later grant can expose records whose changes precede the acknowledged position. Such a profile remains a design gate, not an implicit feature of this complete-feed contract. Authorization failure does not automatically unregister a consumer or release its retention protection; operator removal must be explicit and withdraw the protected replay/completeness promise.

**Repeatability.** A consumer MAY restart from an earlier retained position and receive the same records in the same order under the same source epoch/profile. Delivery is at-least-once. Deduplication uses a versioned immutable event identity, not `(entity kind, entity id, ver, prop id)` alone: lifecycle, metadata and rebind events can collide under that tuple. A repeated identity must carry the same verified semantic payload; a different payload under one identity is corruption/profile failure, never last-write-wins.

For a `change`, the proposed identity is `(sourceEpoch,feedProfile,recordKind,xid,seq)` with exact textual integer carriers. Within an epoch, journal position assignment cannot be reset/reused. Database restore, fork or replacement requires an explicit epoch transition so positions from another source incarnation cannot be mistaken for applied events. The epoch must be trusted deployment metadata, not a caller-selected value on each publish. Retention does not change event identity.

Revision-only, source and reservation events still need distinct stable identities and complete checkpoint ordering in the feed profile. A revision number or record id alone cannot resolve cross-epoch/repeated side-record ambiguity. Until that full event/checkpoint manifest is published, change-position deduplication does not qualify the complete feed. Persist applied-event identity with downstream state atomically; a crash between state and dedup persistence cannot be advertised as exactly-once effects. Payload hashing/encoding, epoch storage/issuance and side-record schema remain D-07 deliverables.

### Proposed complete transaction manifest

#### Candidate PostgreSQL finalization procedure

Native producer guard obligations:

| Entry path | Required checked fact / refusal |
| --- | --- |
| Register member/prerequisite | Installed trusted epoch/profile and actual current top-level xid match the target transaction; qualified native fact exists with exact original bytes and owner pins |
| Repeated registration | Full key/profile/payload equality is required; equal digest with different bytes is corruption, not idempotence |
| Finalize candidate | Only the current producing transaction may change its candidate; independently complete fact set and canonical payload digests must agree; finalized generation cannot be caller-set |
| Direct UPDATE/DELETE/TRUNCATE of stores | Ordinary writer has no mutation path; retention uses a distinct qualified administrative procedure and dependency protection |
| Alter guards/functions/partitions/role rights | Ordinary writer cannot perform it; administrative change invalidates current qualification until coherent installed inventory is refreshed |

Store mutation is not granted merely because a caller may insert canonical graph data. Guarded definer procedures require exact qualified callable identity, secure settings and database-derived caller/transaction context; actor text or a supplied xid is not authority. Cover partition child access, inherited/PUBLIC grants and executable helper paths in CONTRACT-011's installed inventory. A function accepting arbitrary archived payload bytes must verify them against original required facts or refuse; privileged byte storage alone cannot certify feed truth. Trigger/engine registration profiles must prove their entire advertised native writer set, including source/reservation and revision-only paths. Missing guard coverage withdraws native completeness rather than being compensated by reader-side counting.

Candidate fixed native inventory (names are proposed, requiring a new physical profile):

| Store | Native identity / retained facts | Write and retention rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| feed_tx | (source_epoch,feed_profile,xid8); manifest profile, membership generation, finalized generation, exact canonical manifest bytes/digest | Only trusted producer/finalizer mutates its own uncommitted producing xid; committed identity cannot be rewritten by later transactions |
| feed_member | Transaction identity plus qualified native fact kind/key; exact original payload bytes, payload profile/digest, retained owner context; immutable native original fact-write timestamp and fact-clock profile | Same-transaction registration; identical repeated native fact is idempotent, conflicting bytes refuse; ordinal is assigned from complete deterministic order during finalization |
| feed_prerequisite | Transaction identity plus revision/artifact identity; exact definition bytes/pins/digest | No duplicate ambiguous artifact identity; retain alongside any protected dependent manifest/member |
| feed_configuration_prerequisite | Transaction identity plus original source epoch/installation/configuration generation/profile; exact configuration artifact, selected values and producer inventory pin | Original trusted writer admission supplies the facts; retained dependent transactions protect the complete artifact; no current-setting reconstruction |

The candidate fact-clock profile captures the trusted producer statement's server wall-clock write observation at first successful native fact registration, in the producing transaction and its savepoint. Retain exact timestamp/profile with the member. Repeated identical registration retains the original timestamp; finalizer rebuild, forced early finalization, delivery retry and later reads cannot refresh it. Savepoint rollback removes the timestamp with its fact. For changes, the qualified procedure reconciles this capture with original journal write-time semantics; it cannot silently substitute transaction-start time. Revision/source/reservation paths require the same native producer coverage and bypass protection. Authored source timestamps are never timing authority. This proposal defines a write observation, not commit time or xid-derived time. Exact timestamp grammar/precision, statement-clock procedure and journal-equivalence evidence are physical/profile gates.

Freshness computes the minimum retained qualified fact time across required unapplied manifests, after verified seed classification. A cached transaction minimum may be an optimization only when rebuilt atomically from the complete member set and independently checked; late member registration must invalidate it. Native metadata is retained with protected members and its complete association/profile is covered by the freshness observation evidence. Missing timing cannot be reconstructed from today's clock, surviving journal rows or authored payloads. Historical manifests without original timing may remain deliverable under their qualified delivery profile but cannot gain complete freshness qualification by fabricated backfill. The manifest digest continues covering its declared original wire inventory; timing metadata requires an explicit qualified observation/profile association rather than silently changing historical manifest bytes.

The qualified fact key is encoded under the manifest profile, not a locale-dependent JSON string or collision-prone concatenation. Membership generation increments whenever a required member/prerequisite is added; finalized generation must match after reconstruction. This optimization cannot replace verifying complete required fact inventory. Finalization retains exact canonical bytes instead of reconstructing digests from JSONB's altered lexical spelling. All rows live in the enclosing transaction and savepoint rollback restores their prior candidate state. Producer identity and actual xid are database-derived; ordinary caller-supplied epoch/profile text cannot select another transaction's store.

Configuration prerequisite ordering is source epoch, installation ID, numeric configuration generation, then configuration profile identity/version/digest. Compare textual keys by the canonical profile's UTF-8 byte order, never locale collation; compare canonical decimal generation numerically without host-number conversion. Within one epoch/installation/generation, one exact configuration profile/value/inventory artifact is permitted. Repeated identical admission is idempotent; differing profile/value/artifact under that generation is corruption, not an extra prerequisite to sort. A profile must identify whether each member requires configuration and prove complete required closure; an empty array is valid only when no member interpretation requires it. Required artifact equality is exact bytes plus admitted pins, not digest equality alone. Finalizer dirty-generation handling, rollback, ordinary-writer guards and dependency retention cover this fourth store just as the other prerequisite stores. Native inventory qualification must include it.

Committed immutability is enforced by guarded write procedures/privileges and the installed inventory profile; archival/trim is a distinct administrative deletion path under retention arbitration. Protect manifest, member payload and prerequisites together while any consumer/seed/receipt requires them. Partition eligibility is based on complete transaction dependencies, not a manifest timestamp independent of its member partitions. A missing protected row yields unavailable, never a rebuilt complete manifest from surviving rows. Row-home dirty/capacity/seal custody is internal enforcement metadata, not an additional semantic feed fact or mandatory request receipt. Original operation effects readiness precedes row sealing; application/transaction completion verifies all required seals separately. Feed membership/finalization cannot stand in for request-free/no-op/journal-mode-independent operation registry custody, and a future feed manifest digest is not a prerequisite to producing the seal. Empty transactions leave no artificial feed_tx; failed finalization leaves no committed partial store. The four-store source/UMF artifact and explicit/derived identity allocations below now author candidate columns, named constraints, supporting-index expectations and FK references. Complete partition/native-effect composition, bytes carrier/digest procedure, xid/profile admission and guard placement remain B-011b/f design/adoption outputs before installation; no additional UMF capability is assumed.

Use fixed transaction/member storage keyed by trusted source epoch and producing top-level xid. Every participating required fact/prerequisite write registers exact originals and dirties membership in the same transaction/savepoint. Explicit protected finalization performs the bounded complete collection/order/canonical replacement procedure described below. The native deferred completeness constraint validates that resulting complete state without writes or repair. Later membership makes it stale and requires another explicit finalization; repeated guard firing cannot freeze an earlier success or rebuild a manifest implicitly. No committed reader can consume provisional manifest state from an uncommitted transaction.

PostgreSQL 17 documents that constraint triggers may run at transaction end, but [SET CONSTRAINTS](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/sql-set-constraints.html) can force them earlier; [CREATE TRIGGER](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/sql-createtrigger.html) defines their timing. Therefore an early firing is not proof the host transaction is finished. Subsequent writes must register and schedule native validation again. The selected multi-statement finalizer preserves caller timing and may refuse in IMMEDIATE mode; native validation never repairs dirty state or changes constraint timing. Unfinalized/stale state prevents commit, and no incomplete manifest can be published with repair scheduled afterward. Exact trigger placement on partitioned journal paths, security-definer/ordinary-writer privileges, and late writes from other deferred triggers require native review/proof before selecting this candidate.

Finalization checks registered membership against independently required fact paths; the registration table alone cannot prove that a writer failed to register nothing. Enforce registration at each qualified native path, prohibit ordinary tampering with member/manifest storage, and include revision-only acceptance and immutable side insertion. Engine versus trigger journal ownership stays exclusive. Rebuild functions must not recursively generate semantic feed facts or acquire locks outside the reviewed hierarchy. A no-required-fact transaction has no manifest. Legacy rows receive no fabricated original membership. This is a concrete storage/procedure proposal, not installed DDL or a native qualification claim; B-011b owns exact columns, digest implementation, guard inventory and bounded performance proof.

[Draft side-fact binding](bindings/truss-feed-side-record-v0.1.d.ts) preserves exact revision input/report/origin, source creation identity/catalog context with edge endpoint identities, and reservation prior identity/version plus qualified key or endpoint context. Defined source author/at/system members remain optional text/null; omitted differs from explicit null. Source `at` is authored provenance, not commit time. Extension names are unique and cannot collide with defined members; values preserve exact unknown content. Native source objects require lossless projection into this wire shape and back, rather than treating unrecognized content as ignorable. Reservation encoded text is retained with its key-encoding/definition pins; decoding cannot guess a key from the current catalog. Side-fact payloads retain ownership required by CONTRACT-005 after live endpoints disappear.

### Conditional separate-report revision-fact composition

#### Conditional reactivation history closure

ADR-004's reactivation alternative is not admitted by any currently selected feed or seed profile. A schema-valid 0.2.0 acceptance report does not upgrade feed v0.2 or downstream interpretation. If selected, register a distinct exact feed/seed composition that understands the lifecycle report and owner-local key history; older compositions refuse before extraction/application/activation. Do not hide required lifecycle meaning in an unknown extension or silently reinterpret an existing member kind.

For that proposed composition, a retirement/reactivation belongs to its actual revision fact, not a synthetic object event. The revision's prerequisite closure contains the original creation basis and complete ordered lifecycle transition artifacts needed to interpret the resulting key state. Each transition preserves (type_id,key_num), full before/after source definitions and actual retirement references; current key_def cannot substitute for a missing interval. Resolve all artifacts through original protected custody and compare full bytes/identity/profile. Inserting the native history row is a required protected producer path, while exactly one logical revision fact covers the complete acceptance; a history trigger must not create a duplicate revision fact. Actual object_key deletion/reconstruction effects retain their separately selected fact meanings and never count as lifecycle transitions merely because row counts match.

Registration order is complete actual lifecycle/graph/reservation effects and history parity, complete actual event/group metadata, immutable full report construction/parity, revision-fact registration, then head advancement. Enclosing-transaction finalization still admits the complete actual required member/prerequisite inventory. A savepoint rollback removes its lifecycle rows, report, revision fact and corresponding prerequisites together and restores the preceding membership generation. A later rollback or unknown commit retains the original operation/recovery semantics; call-local history/report parity is not commit proof.

Seed extraction at original cut C reconstructs every retained key's lifecycle from its original creation basis through C, including keys currently retired and reserved owner-local numbers with no live object_key rows. The proposed baseline must preserve a complete reconstructible history basis, not only the latest retired_rev flag or a count/digest. Future transitions after C remain excluded. Independently compare extracted current state, history chain, report membership and reservation interpretation in the same original snapshot; full owner-union disclosure and archive retention apply even when the live graph is empty. History preceding a retained anchor may be omitted only under an explicitly adopted complete checkpoint profile whose original evidence proves equivalent interval/source interpretation for every supported historical query; no such checkpoint is supplied here.

Downstream application stages the complete revision report, original definition/lifecycle prerequisites and resulting reservations/state together. Validate chain continuity against the prior admitted key state and qualified ownership before publication. Duplicate delivery compares the original complete fact and artifact bytes; conflicting bytes under the same identity refuse, and neither current definitions nor fresh producer context repairs them. Commit/application proof must include actual durable lifecycle/source inventory as well as graph state. Missing history, unsupported report/lifecycle composition or incomplete protected retention is unavailable, never an empty graph success. Exact new feed/seed profile wires, native producer/extractor/application procedures and adoption remain required design outputs; this conditional ordering does not change existing protocols.

CONTRACT-003's separate immutable report-home option is unadopted. If selected, a revision fact still has the existing FeedRevisionFact shape: exact original acceptance input, complete report artifact and origin under the declared revision/layout/report profiles. Moving report storage does not create a second logical report fact, change feed ordering or authorize a parent-only revision payload. Resolve the report from the declared home with exact original byte/profile custody; no fallback between parent JSONB and the new byte home is allowed. Genesis remains installation/seed evidence, not a positive acceptance fact.

The protected acceptance path must first produce and independently admit all actual rebind event envelopes required by the complete report, including their original mutation-group digest/finalization metadata. A same-transaction event row without required finalized metadata is not sufficient. The selected producer must specify that group's exact membership/finalization boundary; an enclosing host transaction's later feed finalization cannot retroactively supply missing fields in an already immutable report. Separate report storage solves parent-reference direction only, not an unspecified journal group producer. Preserve both journal modes and whole-acceptance semantics; do not guess a digest or narrow the accepted rebind set.

After full event/source/report parity and immutable report insertion, register exactly one complete original revision fact in the same protected original transaction before head publication. Parent insertion may record private required-fact obligations, but cannot finalize a placeholder payload, publish it or rewrite an immutable side artifact later. Full transaction finalization independently verifies every positive acceptance has its complete corresponding revision fact/report and every registered revision fact corresponds to an actual admitted acceptance. Missing, duplicate or mismatched report/source custody prevents completeness and head/acceptance success under the selected protected profile; caller-controlled side bytes cannot repair it. Report construction/storage/registration failure retains whole-acceptance rollback or original unresolved recovery.

Revision-first manifest order is serialization order, not native effect order. The finalizer collects the complete immutable facts after their production and then applies the existing numeric revision/change/source/reservation ordering. No synthetic journal sequence, duplicate report member or early call-local complete manifest follows from that order. A caller-owned transaction may contain later calls/rollback and its final manifest remains governed by the existing top-level transaction protocol. Actual group and feed finalizers retain distinct custody/identities; neither can certify the other's completeness by returning a matching hash alone.

B-006/B-011 must jointly specify the selected complete native registration path and guard coverage before adoption. This option changes neither Weft ABI nor UMF meaning. Legacy conversion requires the same original report/source/event representability and horizon/seed rules as CONTRACT-003; current rows cannot invent an original revision fact.

Candidate manifest order is revision facts first in numeric revision order, then changes in numeric native sequence order, then source facts by entity-kind and numeric typed type/id identity, then reservation facts by entity-kind, numeric type/key number and exact UTF-8 encoded-key bytes. Comparisons use canonical bounded integer tokens and qualified encoding, never host-number rounding or locale collation. This deterministic order satisfies source/reservation delivery after changes while preserving native change order. Duplicate immutable source/reservation primary identities within one producing transaction must resolve to one identical original fact; differing payloads refuse profile completeness rather than choose a last value. Existing storage's immutable single-fact rule does not support repeated overwrites. A future multi-fact profile needs a durable native fact identity and new order/version; reader-generated random IDs or payload-hash identities cannot silently extend this profile.

The producer must finalize this entire member set for the enclosing host transaction, not only one Truss call/savepoint. Later host calls can add facts; rollback removes their tentative membership. Exact native finalization mechanism and guard enforcement remain storage-profile review outputs. A call-local manifest published before host commit cannot certify the complete transaction. Restore/import of legacy rows without retained source/key/history contexts needs an explicit qualified seed horizon or reports unavailable; current data cannot manufacture original side payloads.

[Draft transaction binding](bindings/truss-feed-transaction-v0.1.d.ts) proposes a distinct full-feed domain over committed top-level transactions. Each nonempty producing transaction has an immutable manifest containing every required change/revision/source/reservation fact, exact payload-profile/digest pins, ordered member ordinals and complete catalog prerequisite artifacts. A revision-only transaction is nonempty even without graph changes. Transactions with no required feed facts create no artificial mutation record. Original native journal sequence remains a change's key; manifest ordinal is separate feed ordering metadata, never a fabricated journal sequence.

Candidate member identity is source epoch, complete-feed profile, producing xid and manifest ordinal. Ordinals are contiguous canonical nonnegative integer text starting at zero; members are unique by ordinal. Stable native keys support traceability, but a repeated side fact cannot be deduplicated solely by current object/reservation identity. Producer assignment of ordering and any side identity must be persisted atomically with original effects under the reviewed profile. Reader enumeration cannot assign a new identity on every replay. Same member identity with changed payload or manifest is corruption. The manifest digest covers its version/context/xid/profile, ordered key/payload pins and prerequisite artifacts, excluding its own digest field, under a distinct proposed `truss-feed-manifest/0.1.0` canonical domain.

Source manifest keys include entity kind plus typed identity. Reservation keys expose the native `(entityKind, typeId, keyNumber, encodedKey)` tuple rather than an unreviewed opaque identity string. Runtime admission verifies each key against its decoded side payload, including object-key versus edge-endpoint kind, definition pins and profile-specific key-number rules. A structurally valid tuple cannot establish historical ownership or payload correspondence. [Negative binding cases](bindings/truss-feed-transaction-v0.1.typecheck.ts) reject abbreviated reservation keys, host-number xids, journal/complete-feed domain substitution, empty missing-member lists and unavailable outcomes with complete payloads; they prove type distinctions only.

Native production must prove manifest completeness in the original transaction across every qualified write path, including trigger/engine modes and revision-only acceptance. A reader cannot manufacture completeness by counting whichever rows remain after retention. Existing rows lacking a qualified manifest are unqualified for this profile; migration/backfill cannot infer missing past facts from current rows. Manifest storage, unavoidable production, exact side payload schema and ordinal assignment are design decisions still required before this candidate can be accepted.

Configuration prerequisites are distinct from catalog-revision artifacts. When member interpretation requires original mutation configuration, retain its exact archived artifact and selected source-epoch/installation/generation/profile/value pins in `configurationPrerequisites`. Verify manifest context, artifact correspondence and trusted original batch/writer admission; selected metadata alone does not prove admission. Include the complete ordered prerequisite inventory in canonical manifest bytes and fragment byte budgets, retain it with every protected dependent transaction and stage it atomically with downstream application. Missing or conflicting required configuration refuses completion. A later configuration cannot repair the gap or reinterpret an old reservation/result. Configuration-only administrative replication is not invented as a graph mutation by this prerequisite carrier; a profile advertising that replication needs its own explicit event/baseline design.

Fragments repeat the immutable manifest and carry ordinal-addressed exact payload artifacts. Assemble all declared members, verify bytes/digests/profile, prerequisite availability and current historical authorization before exposing a complete applicable transaction. Duplicate identical fragment members are idempotent; conflicting duplicates, undeclared ordinals or missing required members refuse completion. Resource overflow is explicit unavailable, never truncated success. No fragment is an applied checkpoint. Large transactions may require a qualified durable staging protocol; application/dedup/final boundary must be atomic at the downstream or follow its reviewed durable proof protocol.

The proposed complete boundary `(context, xid, manifestSha256)` acknowledges all required members and prerequisites of that transaction and all preceding required transactions after the prior boundary. Read transaction xids in qualified safe-watermark order, not inferred commit timestamp order. Observe watermark and manifest/payloads coherently; authorization gaps or retained-history gaps block advancement. Catalog prerequisites may be staged earlier, but staging a future definition cannot acknowledge its producing transaction or replace a missing revision record. Complete-feed boundaries are not interchangeable with journal-only `(xid, seq)` checkpoints. Registration/seed starting boundaries, empty-feed representation, worker fencing and cross-store acknowledgment proof remain explicit D-07 outputs.

**Consumer positions.** A consumer registers a name in `feed_consumer` and updates its row with the `(xid, seq)` of the last record it has durably applied, in the same transaction or step that applied it where the downstream allows. A position ahead of the safe watermark is not an error: the consumer reads nothing until the watermark passes it.

### Candidate manifest discovery

[Discovery binding](bindings/truss-feed-discovery-v0.1.d.ts) returns the first eligible required producing transaction after a qualified complete resume boundary, or a proven empty source interval. It reads committed manifest membership and the safe watermark coherently under the selected observation profile. Validate current registration/generation/context and resolve the full seed activation/classifier or prior manifest/coverage evidence before enumeration; a caller-supplied boundary is not authority or proof. Seed resumes begin at the persisted inclusive xmin and still discover represented transactions for classifier/coverage accounting. Transaction resumes exclude the already covered transaction; coverage resumes include the frontier transaction. No subtract-one xid or invented seq maximum is allowed.

Return the lowest eligible required xid under the qualified native xid order, including revision-only and side-only transactions, with its complete immutable manifest/prerequisites. Do not skip an inaccessible or missing required transaction to reveal a later one. Current historical authority must cover the manifest's complete retained owner union before disclosure, including prerequisite artifacts. Missing protected inventory is unavailable, not an empty interval. Resource bounds cover enumeration and complete manifest bytes; exhausting either bound refuses without partial manifest or fabricated end. The native profile must detect missing required membership using its qualified producer/retention inventory, rather than assuming the surviving manifest table proves completeness.

An empty interval names exact inclusive start and exclusive frontier, bounded by the observed safe watermark and backed by protected source enumeration evidence. Zero width means no progress. A checkpoint already beyond this observation's watermark cannot produce a reversed interval or new coverage: refuse observation and retry a qualified fresh scope. The evidence proves source enumeration only; it cannot certify committed downstream application, retire a seed classifier, advance protection or serve as a VerifiedApplication. Consumers combine it with the existing interval/application protocol. Registration, proof verification, seed activation and complete-feed freshness remain separate outputs; discovery does not spawn workers or change any checkpoint. Native xid/snapshot/evidence grammar and bounded enumeration procedure require review and qualification.

### Checkpoint concurrency and authority

Advancement requires authenticated consumer scope/epoch/profile and an expected prior checkpoint. Serialize the compare-and-set on the registered consumer row. A matching prior checkpoint may advance only to a complete durably applied boundary; equal position is an idempotent acknowledgment with no fabricated application progress. A different observed prior checkpoint returns checkpoint conflict and the permitted current state, not an unconditional overwrite. A reverse position refuses; replay from an earlier delivery cursor does not move the durable applied checkpoint backward.

The acknowledged boundary must cover every required event in its interval, including transaction completeness and side-record obligations under the selected profile. A high journal position alone does not prove that coverage. An already stored position ahead of a particular observation's safe watermark can be represented without error; this is not authorization to submit speculative future positions. New acknowledgments require actual delivered/applied evidence and compatible epoch/scope. Registration, reset and reseed are separately authorized administrative operations, never implicit consequences of an unknown consumer name or stale worker token.

Multiple downstream workers must either share an atomic application/dedup/checkpoint transaction or publish a qualified durable application proof protocol. Updating the source checkpoint before downstream durability can lose changes and is forbidden. When stores cannot commit atomically, preserve at-least-once replay through durable downstream deduplication; do not claim distributed atomicity from sequential acknowledgments. Checkpoint updated_at changes only on qualified acknowledgment according to its declared semantics; a heartbeat has a separate identity and cannot make unapplied work appear fresh. The public wire schema, worker fencing and cross-store proof protocol remain explicit feed-profile deliverables.

### Proposed cross-store worker protocol

[Host proof-verifier registration](bindings/truss-feed-proof-verifier-v0.1.d.ts) is an explicit optional assembly host service bound to exact feed capability, verifier and downstream profile pins. Capture immutable registration metadata at inert construction; conflicting duplicate registrations refuse construction. Absence leaves feed reading available under its own profile but acknowledgment unavailable before checkpoint SQL. No global verifier registry, implicit network discovery or default trust profile exists. Host verifier lifetime remains host-owned and assembly disposal cannot terminate it.

The host invokes `assess` separately from source acknowledgment. Assessment observes registered downstream evidence custody, actual committed application and complete interval under its declared profile; an unavailable assessment issues no capability. `recognizes` checks the registered issuer's custody of the exact immutable returned capability/proof, rather than a supplied marker or matching digest. Custody cannot be reconstructed by deserializing JSON, and recognition alone is not current source authority. Acknowledgment still checks current worker generation/registration, exact expected prior and complete boundary interval under source serialization. Evidence remains immutable after issuance; a host mutation of submitted/returned objects invalidates recognition. Lost issuer custody requires fresh qualified assessment of the same durable application; it cannot reset downstream state or assume nonapplication. Verification service errors produce unavailable assessment and never a synthetic verified result. Profile selection must define bounded evidence resolution, current access and replay/reconciliation across restart; this API does not prove a downstream adapter qualifies.


[Draft feed facade](bindings/truss-feed-capability-v0.1.d.ts) exposes transaction-affine fragment reading and source acknowledgment. Selection is inert. `readFragment` validates the exact manifest, delivery profile, continuation, limits, retention and current historical authority before returning any payload. A supplied manifest/cursor is a locator, not evidence that the transaction committed or that its members are visible. Verify source membership, safe-watermark eligibility and complete prerequisites in the coherent selected observation. End-of-fragment delivery cannot establish downstream application or advance source protection.

`acknowledgeInTransaction` accepts the existing host-verified application capability and revalidates its issuer, registration/generation, exact context and expected prior boundary at the serialized source checkpoint transition. A cast or serialized marker cannot acquire verifier authority. It requires a verified writable live scope; fragment reads support the separately qualified read-only observation profile. Advanced/equal results are pending in the supplied transaction, even when that scope is engine-owned by an outer callback. Source retention advances only on confirmed checkpoint commit; equal acknowledgment does not invent progress. A conflict returns current state only after current consumer disclosure authorization; hidden state returns unavailable without a boundary. Native/transaction failures use the executor outcome rather than claiming a failed compare-and-set proved no checkpoint effect. No method commits, retries the host transaction, starts a downstream worker, verifies through caller-selected network locations or constructs a compiler.

These two methods cover retained-manifest delivery and acknowledgment only. Independent interval-coverage verification, seed activation and administrative orchestration remain callable design outputs. Registration/generation, proof-verifier, downstream installation and complete freshness declarations are now authored separately; their exact wire/evidence/native profiles remain open. Journal-only freshness/checkpoint types cannot substitute for complete-feed boundaries. Native CAS, retention arbitration and cross-store proof profiles remain unqualified design gates.


[Draft worker/proof binding](bindings/truss-feed-worker-v0.1.d.ts) distinguishes source claim, downstream installation, submitted proof, host-verified proof and acknowledgment. Worker identity includes context, consumer id, trusted registration id and exact generation text. Remove/recreate of a consumer name uses a new registration identity; old workers/proofs cannot acquire authority through name equality. Applied boundary is either qualified seed activation or complete transaction, rather than a fabricated xid/sequence for an empty seed. Seed activation evidence is a reference to the separately required full activation protocol, not proof supplied by the caller.

The opaque verified-proof TypeScript marker prevents accidental assignment in supported code but does not establish trust against casts, forged JavaScript or deserialized content. Runtime acknowledgment accepts verification only from the host's registered verifier/context and checks current registration/generation, exact prior/result interval, downstream identity/profile, artifact integrity and trusted durable application observation. Never deserialize a caller's `verified` marker into acknowledgment authority. Wire content and verifier capability remain separate. Unknown required proof fields/profile versions refuse; evidence resolution uses authorized host stores and cannot initiate arbitrary network requests.

Propose a monotonically increasing administrative worker generation per registered consumer/context. Acquiring a generation serializes on its source consumer row and invalidates acknowledgment authority for prior generations; a caller cannot nominate its generation. Expiry of a wall-clock lease alone is not write fencing. Before a new worker applies anything, the downstream must atomically install that generation in its consumer application state, refusing a lower generation. Every downstream application transaction checks the installed generation and persists complete transaction effects, event deduplication identities, manifest digest and durable applied boundary together. If the downstream cannot provide that atomic boundary, this profile is unsupported; external nontransactional effects require their own qualified idempotent protocol.

Source generation acquisition and downstream installation are not a distributed commit. An old worker may finish a transaction before the new generation is installed downstream, but it cannot commit application after the downstream fencing barrier under the qualified concurrency protocol. The replacement reads the downstream durable boundary after installing its generation and reconciles it with the source checkpoint; it does not discard already applied state or assume source and downstream are equal. Crashing between acquisition and downstream installation leaves no permission for the replacement to apply unfenced work. A higher installed downstream generation requires explicit reconciliation, never downgrade.

After downstream commit, a host-established proof verifier observes the authenticated downstream consumer/context/generation, prior and resulting complete boundaries, manifest/interval integrity and committed application evidence. Caller-submitted digests, source delivery tokens or self-declared committed flags are insufficient. The verifier must have a declared trust/access profile and cannot load an arbitrary proof URL or caller code. Only then may source acknowledgment compare-and-set the expected prior boundary under the same registered generation. If source acknowledgment fails or its response is lost, retain downstream state and replay/reconcile idempotently; never roll it back by assuming the source update failed. Source retention remains protected by the older source checkpoint until acknowledgment is confirmed.

Reset/reseed/removal increments or retires generation under shared retention arbitration, with explicit downstream transition obligations. Proof from a different epoch/profile/scope/consumer/generation cannot advance a checkpoint. An authenticated downstream boundary ahead of source may support interval reconciliation only when every required intervening manifest and prerequisite has durable coverage; a highest xid alone is insufficient. Exact registration/generation wire, downstream locking/isolation adapter requirements, proof-verifier output schema and seed transition remain design outputs. This proposed protocol does not claim universal cross-store atomicity or activate workers in the current layout.

**Freshness evidence.** For one consumer, the age of the feed is the time since the earliest change that the consumer has not yet applied was written:

```sql
SELECT now() - min(at) AS lag
FROM truss.journal j, truss.feed_consumer c
WHERE c.consumer = $1 AND (j.xid, j.seq) > (c.xid, c.seq) AND j.xid < pg_snapshot_xmin(pg_current_snapshot());
```

A change written but still above the safe watermark counts as not yet publishable and is reported separately. A publisher MUST make the consumer's position and its own `updated_at` readable by whoever relies on the copy, so a reader of the copy can say "reflects the source through position P".

### Freshness interpretation

The [draft TypeScript observation binding](bindings/truss-feed-observation-v0.1.d.ts) makes present/empty/unavailable backlog and non-disclosing checkpoint outcomes explicit, with exact position/duration text. It deliberately labels its checkpoint domain journal-only; this inspection artifact cannot close full feed requirements for revision-only/side records. It supplies no acknowledgment mutation method; the separate complete-feed facade and host proof/worker declarations now define proposed acknowledgment/fencing boundaries, with native qualification pending. Runtime validation must establish integer/timestamp grammar, nonnegative ages, context equality, snapshot coherence and authority; TypeScript structural compatibility is insufficient. [Negative binding examples](bindings/truss-feed-observation-v0.1.typecheck.ts) pass strict no-emit checking for numeric position rejection, unavailable-zero rejection and unauthorized checkpoint disclosure rejection. This is type evidence only, not native behavior.

Freshness observations name the source epoch/profile/scope, actual durable applied checkpoint, checkpoint update time, observation time and safe watermark from one qualified snapshot. Publishable backlog is either `present` with oldest write time and exact nonnegative age, `empty` with no oldest time and age zero, or `unavailable` with a permitted reason. Empty publishable backlog does not mean globally caught up: committed rows held above the watermark are a separately observed backlog, and uncommitted changes cannot be counted by this snapshot.

Unknown consumers, missing retained history, unauthorized/incomplete scope, incompatible epochs and failed required observation produce unavailable, never empty/zero. Scope-level unavailable reasons respect CONTRACT-005 disclosure limits; hidden timestamps/counts are not exposed as diagnostics. A checkpoint ahead of the watermark describes the stored position without proving source completeness. Journal-only freshness must declare that it excludes revision-only and side-record obligations until those positions are defined.

Use one server observation clock consistently; transaction-start now() in a long adopted transaction cannot stand in for current observation time. Durations use exact integer text under a declared unit/profile, without floating-point rounding. A clock anomaly yielding a negative age is unavailable with a clock diagnostic, not clamped into apparent catch-up. The qualified envelope must retain the observation context needed to interpret these states; publisher heartbeat age is a separate measure and cannot replace unapplied-change age.

**Retention.** A journal partition MUST NOT be dropped while it holds a row past the lowest position of any registered consumer, unless that consumer has been removed from `feed_consumer` by an operator. A deployment with no registered consumer retains by its own policy.

Retention eligibility and consumer lifecycle share a versioned administrative serialization boundary. Under that boundary, enumerate the exact candidate partition's required event positions, registered consumer protection, retained reconstruction baselines/definitions and any replay dependencies; revalidate immediately before transactional drop. A timestamp cutoff alone is insufficient because transaction positions and partition write times have different orderings. Consumer registration/reset/reseed and operator removal must participate in the same arbitration, so a newly protected boundary cannot race with a stale trim plan.

Publish the resulting retained horizon atomically with a successful transactional drop. A failed drop leaves the horizon unchanged; a successful drop cannot be repaired by advertising an empty replacement partition as restored evidence. A consumer requesting an already unavailable boundary refuses or enters a qualified reseed protocol, never registers as if complete replay were possible. Acknowledgment may safely advance protection only after qualified durable application. Request receipts protect journal dependencies if their selected persistence needs them; self-contained receipts must prove that independence rather than assume it.

Exact maintenance lock identity, required-event inventory, protected horizon representation and administrative wire API remain design deliverables. A retention job cannot claim this race guarantee until all lifecycle paths use the profile. Native tests must use barriers between eligibility scan, registration/acknowledgment and drop, with independent partition/horizon observations; wall-clock sleeps do not establish ordering.

**Schema changes.** The `revision` records give the consumer every document of every revision in order, so a consumer can learn new types before the first change that uses them. A consumer that does not support a document's UMF version stops at that revision and reports it; it does not skip it.

## Precedence and Compatibility

### Transaction fragment paging

Ordinals are the manifest's contiguous zero-based canonical decimal tokens. First starts at ordinal 0; after N starts at N+1, compared with exact integer arithmetic. Choose the largest consecutive prefix fitting both record and byte budgets. Byte measurement uses CONTRACT-009's canonical UTF-8 tree encoding of the complete final response, including the actual more/end cursor and canonical base64 artifact bytes. Whitespace choices, compression, HTTP framing and decoded payload size cannot change this admitted bound. Measure each candidate's final continuation shape; omit no manifest/prerequisite bytes to force a fit. The selected delivery profile pins this encoding and measurement procedure. A final-page end may fit differently from a more page, so compute final shape before deciding the maximal prefix.

The draft fragment request/page/cursor types in [transaction binding](bindings/truss-feed-transaction-v0.1.d.ts) page one pinned transaction's complete manifest in ordinal order. Verify context, producing xid, complete manifest equality/digest and delivery profile on every resume. afterOrdinal is a delivery cursor only; it never advances source acknowledgment or retention protection. Limit values are positive canonical decimal text within the selected delivery profile; changing limits within that profile preserves selection and ordinal boundary. Reject an ordinal outside the manifest, wrong epoch or changed manifest payload.

Available pages contain a nonempty consecutive range of manifest members after the boundary. Encoded-byte accounting includes the complete manifest, prerequisites, payloads and response envelope, not only raw record bytes. More requires at least one remaining manifest member, and end means all members were delivered under that manifest, not that durable downstream application succeeded. If even one next member plus required envelope cannot fit, return resource_limit without a partial member or invented end. A qualified staged/blob transport requires its own profile with verified complete artifact retrieval; URLs or caller-sized truncation are not implicit support. Assembly independently checks all ordinals/digests/prerequisites before atomic application. Duplicate page delivery is harmless only through that verified assembly/idempotent application protocol.

Resume revalidates current historical authorization and retained availability. A cursor does not hold a snapshot or renew protection by itself; the registration/seed/retention protocol must preserve the original required evidence. Missing retained members, hidden required payloads or a changed source epoch produce unavailable and no fragment acknowledgment. Repeating the same admitted boundary over immutable retained content returns the same member selection for the same limits; context loss refuses instead of silently reopening under different policy.

### Administrative consumer transitions

The [recovery storage allocation](feed-recovery-storage-allocation.proposal.md) specifies separate immutable administrative receipts and retained seed attempt/artifact homes, including retry ordering and source/downstream transaction boundaries. These homes are absent from the 0.6 review DDL.

[Draft administration binding](bindings/truss-feed-administration-v0.1.d.ts) makes reseed/removal expected-state operations under the same retention exclusion as trimming. Verify exact context, registration identity, generation and expected applied boundary before effects. A copied worker identity or asserted reason does not establish administrative authority. A mismatch returns conflict without modifying generation, checkpoint or protection. Persist request identity, exact verified input and immutable audit outcome atomically with source-side changes; repeated request IDs require complete input equality, not digest-only equality. Uncertain commit reconciles the original transition before another attempt.

Begin-reseed advances worker fencing generation and registers an explicit seed attempt while preserving the old applied boundary and all its protection. It cannot set an arbitrary new checkpoint or release old evidence. CONTRACT-006's downstream installation/activation proof determines the later replacement; a fresh seed attempt is not proof of durable activation. Remove-consumer has two explicit modes. Fenced removal verifies qualified downstream evidence that old generations cannot commit new application. Withdrawal removal instead requires trusted administrative authorization explicitly accepting loss of protected replay/completeness for the abandoned copy; it reports unverified_abandoned and makes no downstream fencing claim. In either mode retire source registration/generation and release only that consumer's protection atomically. Inactivity, a timeout or an ordinary worker request cannot authorize withdrawal. Name reuse creates a fresh registration identity; old workers cannot acknowledge or renew source protection. Other consumers, pending seeds and receipt protections remain independently protected; never drop a partition merely because one consumer was removed. Administrative audit preserves the chosen mode, prior/final generations, boundary and verified authorization without unauthorized disclosure.

Withdrawal supports recovery when a downstream target is permanently unreachable. Its old worker might still apply already-held data, so that copy cannot be presented as a current complete replica or rejoin under its retired registration. Rejoining requires a new registration and qualified reseed/fencing protocol. Do not fabricate application proof or advance the abandoned checkpoint to make removal appear caught up.

Administrative receipt identity is (trusted sourceEpoch, administrativeNamespace, requestId), independent of consumer display name and worker generation. Persist full exact request, successful semantic result, actual database role and verified authorization evidence in the same source transaction as the transition. Do not persist commit_unknown, unavailable or conflict as a successful transition. Serialize request arbitration with retention administration before reading expected worker state. After current administrative authorization and full-input equality checks, an existing committed receipt returns its original result even though the original generation is now stale; checking expected generation first would make a successful retry look like a new conflict. Never recompute a reseed generation or reconstruct a removed outcome from current consumer absence.

Missing receipt permits a fresh transition only after the original uncertain transaction is known terminated and coherent observation proves absence. Pending visibility or an unreachable observer remains unknown. Receipt lookup cannot silently elevate roles or disclose prior withdrawal details. Retain compact nonreusable request identity if payload archival expires; expired input/result cannot replay and must return an explicit unavailable profile outcome rather than re-execute under the same identity. Exact storage/schema and administrative archive policy are proposed design outputs under B-011f, separate from group-request receipts in ADR-005.

Artifact references resolve only through authorized stores and cannot fetch code or establish trust by themselves. Documentation agreement is design evidence, not native qualification.

Candidate `truss-seed-snapshot/0.1.0` captures one fixed PostgreSQL snapshot S and retains its exact full transaction identifiers xmin, xmax and in-progress xid set. All catalog, graph, source and reservation extraction runs under S, with the supported policy scope. Keep the exporting transaction alive until every importing reader has established that snapshot; expiration refuses rather than falling back to current reads. Snapshot export/import permissions, lifetime limits and target-version support need adapter qualification.

Register retention protection before extraction for all required feed evidence with xid at or above S.xmin. For journal changes, resume inclusively at xid S.xmin, not after the greatest visible journal row. Feed eligibility still uses the current safe watermark. For each complete transaction envelope T, classify it against S: T is already represented when xid(T) is below S.xmax and is not in S's in-progress set; otherwise apply it after seed activation. Transactions below S.xmin are already represented and need not be replayed. Compare full exact xid values, never host numbers or truncated transaction IDs. This classification handles an older in-progress host transaction that writes Truss only after S and a newer committed transaction already included in S.

Do not classify individual rows against newly observed snapshots. A transaction's required changes, source/reservation events and revision effects share one seed-representation decision. A represented transaction can advance replay progress only after its complete required envelope is accounted for; an unrepresented transaction must be applied atomically under the selected downstream protocol. Persist S's visibility evidence with the seed manifest until catch-up passes its transition boundary. This avoids replaying an old create/delete over seeded state without requiring a guessed per-record baseline version for deleted records.

The candidate requires every required event to expose its producing full xid or an equivalent proved transaction association, including revision-only and side records. Current feed storage does not yet establish that association for all kinds. Therefore this is a concrete proposed algorithm, not an installed or fully qualified profile. D-07 must resolve transaction envelope association, inclusive checkpoint representation, retention registration, seed activation/fencing and native snapshot evidence before implementation closeout. If any required event lacks association, refuse this profile rather than drop it or attach an invented xid.

### Proposed seed activation state machine

Extract all required baseline surfaces under the same qualified snapshot. Validate complete inventory, exact artifact digests, catalog/definition ownership, resource/profile compatibility and server snapshot fidelity before marking staged. A stage is private and cannot replace the old active copy. An expired snapshot during extraction or missing protection invalidates staging and requires a fresh seed; no partial baseline is completed from unrelated later reads.

The downstream installs a staged seed by one atomic activation transaction under its installed worker generation: verify the immutable seed identity/pins, install the complete baseline, persist the seed-aware visibility/resume state and switch the active-copy pointer together. Old active state is retained until this succeeds. If physical baseline loading is too large for one transaction, a qualified shadow generation may be populated beforehand, but pointer activation and verified generation/manifest state are atomic; ordinary readers cannot observe partial shadow state. External side effects do not inherit atomic activation.

Only a trusted downstream observation of committed activation may allow source registration to transition to the active seed boundary by generation/expected-state compare-and-set. This transition preserves inclusive xmin replay protection; it does not mark all visible transactions as acknowledged or use the maximum visible xid as a checkpoint. A crash after downstream activation but before source confirmation re-observes the same durable seed identity and completes idempotent confirmation. It cannot reload the baseline over newer downstream replay state. An unknown commit remains unknown until qualified observation under CONTRACT-007, not guessed from dispatch success.

Replay from inclusive xmin uses the persisted snapshot classifier for every complete producing transaction: represented transactions advance verified interval coverage without reapplying facts; unrepresented transactions apply atomically under the worker protocol. Do not discard the classifier merely because the first transaction was applied. Retirement of seed-aware replay state requires verified coverage of every eligible required transaction through the snapshot's uncertainty range and a coherent safe-watermark/empty-interval observation; absence of an event at xmax is not a gap or a fabricated transaction boundary. The exact interval-proof schema and handoff to ordinary transaction checkpoints remain review outputs. Protection moves only after confirmed source acknowledgment of complete coverage.

Abandonment is an explicit authorized transition under retention arbitration. It invalidates the attempt's worker/activation authority and removes only its own staged resources; it cannot delete the prior active copy or implicitly release that copy's consumer protection. A lease timeout may request review but cannot by itself publish a new seed or advance checkpoints. Once downstream activation is committed, cleanup must reconcile its actual active generation before treating an attempt as abandoned. Full seed wire/resource profiles, native locking and qualified activation adapter evidence remain D-07 outputs; these states specify a candidate protocol rather than installed support.

A seed profile must bind one consistent catalog/graph/source/reservation snapshot, source epoch/scope and a resume boundary with a proof that every required change is either represented in the seed or delivered afterward. Copying tables under unrelated READ COMMITTED statements and recording the latest journal position afterward is insufficient. Define how catalog-head barriers, exported/fixed snapshots, event eligibility and baseline version evidence compose in the selected profile; none alone proves the cut. The exact algorithm, manifest wire format and activation/fencing protocol remain D-07 deliverables. These obligations preserve the full seed requirement rather than qualifying a snapshot-only demonstration.

## Error Semantics

| Condition | Outcome | Retry | Recovery |
|-----------|---------|-------|----------|
| A consumer's position is older than the retained journal | the consumer cannot resume and reports it | no | Re-seed the copy from a snapshot, then resume from the snapshot's position |
| A document's UMF version is unsupported by the consumer | the consumer stops at that revision | after upgrade | Upgrade the consumer |
| A publisher delivers a record out of order | the consumer must detect it by position and stop | no | Fix the publisher |
| A long-running transaction holds the safe watermark back | later changes are delayed; lag grows | wait | End or cancel the transaction |

## Examples

```text
position (1042, 7):  change  o 1043 type 7 ver 2 update prop 12 "partial" -> "substantial" rev 3 origin {actor:"a", db_role:"w"}
position (1042, 8):  source  o 1043 load "load-1" {author:"x"}
revision 3 is delivered before the first change with rev 3.
```

## Non-Normative Notes

The feed's end-to-end freshness depends on the transport. A database-native synchronization to a warehouse may deliver changes within seconds; that figure is the transport's to establish and measure, and this contract only requires that the lag be observable. The consumer watermark's cost is that one slow transaction delays everything behind it.
